# v6 Task S — buffer sweep: is a valid Clock metric reachable?

Generated by `analysis_v6_taskS_sweep.py` from
`results_v6/taskS_buffer_sweep.csv` and
`results_v6/taskS_buffer_confirm.csv`.

Both parameterisations in the main Task S run fail the validity
check, so this sweep asks whether *any* buffer length yields a
`clock_r` that actually measures division regularity. One variable
only: sample count fixed at 120, span varied.

Runs: 4 arms x 40 seeds, 40,000 ticks each, 1D engine.

## The curve

Validity requires rho(clock_r, CV) to be **negative** (more regular
-> higher autocorrelation -> lower CV) and significant.

| buffer (ticks) | periods spanned | defined at tick | rho | p | valid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,200 | 0.47 | 1,200 | -0.0354 | 0.8305 | no |
| 6,000 | 2.36 | 6,000 | +0.2162 | 0.1862 | no |
| 12,000 | 4.71 | 12,000 | -0.0245 | 0.8823 | no |
| 24,000 | 9.43 | 24,000 | -0.3822 | 0.0163 | no |

Bonferroni threshold over 4 arms: p < 0.0125.

Two features of this table carry the argument.

**Definedness equals buffer length exactly**, in every arm. This is
not an empirical finding but an identity: `clock_r` is defined the
moment a cell's history buffer fills, so its definedness time *is*
the buffer span. Whatever span validity demands is therefore also
the earliest the metric can exist.

**The approach to validity is not monotone.** The 2.36-period arm
returns rho = +0.2162 — the *wrong sign* — and the
4.71-period arm -0.0245, indistinguishable from
zero. Only the widest arm separates from noise. We therefore do not
claim a smooth relationship between span and validity; at n = 40 the
intermediate arms are simply uninformative.

## Out-of-sample confirmation

The widest arm was selected on seeds 0-39 with p = 0.0163,
which does **not** clear the Bonferroni threshold of 0.0125. A
borderline, multiplicity-uncorrected result is not an acceptable
basis for this paper's central claim, so the arm was re-run on 40
seeds it had never been evaluated on.

| set | seeds | n | rho | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| selection | 0-39 | 39 | -0.3822 | 0.01635 |
| confirmation | 40-79 | 40 | -0.4565 | 0.00307 |

The confirmation is a single pre-specified test of a directional
prediction, so it needs no correction: rho = -0.4565,
p = 0.00307. `clock_r` became defined at tick
24,000 in 40/40 runs.

## What this establishes

A `clock_r` that measurably tracks division regularity requires a
buffer of about 9.4 division periods
(~24,000 ticks at a median period of 2,546).
Because definedness equals buffer length, that is also the earliest
tick at which a valid Clock reading can exist.

| metric | defined at | ratio to S |
|---|---|---|
| S (Map), spatial correlation | 150 | — |
| CV (original Clock) | 4,450 | 30x |
| clock_r, **valid** parameterisation | **24,000** | **160x** |

The redesign was built specifically to remove the definedness floor
that produced defect 3. Made valid, it does not merely fail to
remove the floor — it raises it, from 4,450 ticks to
24,000, a factor of 160 above the
spatial metric rather than the original 30x.

This is the quantitative form of the time-frequency constraint. A
regularity statistic needs several periods of the process it
describes; a spatial correlation needs one field relaxation time
(~40 ticks) and is defined by ~150. No estimator choice closes a
gap of that size, because the gap is the ratio of the two
phenomena's intrinsic timescales.

**Caveat, stated plainly.** At a 24,000-tick buffer in a
40,000-tick run, `clock_r` is an average over cells that have lived
24,000 ticks — founder-lineage cells only, since daughters start
with an empty buffer (`Cell.create`, `age = 0`) while mothers retain
theirs. The valid measurement is therefore made on an age-selected
subpopulation. This does not rescue the metric: a cell too young to
have a full buffer is exactly a cell whose period has not yet been
observed. The selection restates the constraint rather than
concealing it.
